Hey anyone reading, Sonicnathan 1 or as I normally go by these days, KingofKoopas here.

Thank you for downloading this level pack. This pack started production over 16 years ago and although I never had the time or motivation to finish it, I put my everything into it. 
This document is more of the ramblings of someone who's regretted never finishing this pack, explaining some of my design decisions and just once and for all clearing my head of all 
the thoughts I've had on this pack. If you don't care about any of that, I hope you enjoy the levels. For those that do:

Episode 2 was part of what was going to be a 4 episode pack. I took great inspiration from Evilmike's Invasion of Deserto series and was trying to create something of that scale. 
Being I was 14 or so at the time I didn't understand the level of time and effort that took, nor did I have the skills to do so. Still I started making this immediately after
Episode 1, which I later went back and completely re-designed after I gained more level design experience. Over the years I slowly picked away at finishing it but was never able to
reach a point I called it "done". As to why?
1: I was never happy with what I had done. Every time I said I was done with a level, I wanted to go back and do more, add more, make it longer, add more features. I could never
meet my own impossible expectations. After years and years of doing this, I burned myself out and just lost all motivation.
2: I had trouble finding people to help me test, this was a minor issue but still a lack of any feedback besides my own made point 1 even worse.
3: JJ2+. Angelscripting is in my opinion the greatest feature of JJ2+, but it changed the whole playfield in my eyes. With what you could now do, the standards for levels I felt
were way higher then when I first started making levels. Going back to point 1, I felt I NEEDED to learn Angelscript to make what I truly wanted to. By that point however, my life
was starting to get busy and I didn't have time to learn a coding language. 
And so this pack sat on my hardrive for years and every now and then I'd think about going back and finishing it. By this point I just don't have the time or motivation to finish
this or the other 2 episodes I wanted to make. So instead everything the follows is going to be the story I had planned, thoughts on each level and what I had planned that I never got around to
doing. I recommend reading this after playing the pack.

-Down Town Chaos-
This level just kept getting longer and longer the more I edited it. Originally the idea was I wanted each level to be at least 10 minutes long. Since this level is just one long corridor shooter, I just
kept adding section after section until I reach the width limit of the level. If I re-did this level I would have leaned more into the verticality and puzzle aspects of later levels. 
This level is actually the reason I made the Colonial Fix tileset all those years ago. Working with this tileset by default just SUCKS.

-Fight Night Street-
This level is where I started really experimenting with new ideas. I found that the Queen boss created an invisible wall which could be used to block the player. Since knocking the queen off a ledge
only ends the level if she hits a spring and doesn't need an activate boss event, it could be used as a guard blocking access until defeated. The frog sections is an area I wish I re-did.
These whole sections started after I learned you could push a crate while it was in the air, which I attempted to use to make a platforming challenge. However even now I think it just drags out
and I should have focused on dodging enemies and just making a more traditional platforming challenge. Maybe should have seen if the frog's tongue can activate tigger zones. Fun fact, the boss
fight in this level was one of the last things I made for this pack and was added after the final proper level, baddy breeding, was done. It should go without saying I took serious inspiration
from the bossfight in the fortress of winds from The Invasion of Deserto Episode 5.

-House of Shells-
This is where I started experimenting with an entirely different style of levels which I ended up liking. The idea was that you had a bunch of objectives in an open ended level that you could tackle
in any order. As a kid for some reason I thought linear level design was bad and wanted to get away from it. I also attempted a more ambient level making more use of lighting effects. However this level
suffered from one major issue, which was secrecy. As a player how are you supposed to know you can buttstomp a random block to continue? Old versions had no hints on secret passages, holes in the walls,
crate locations ect. Over the year I went back and added arrows as hints, which to me feels kinda cheap but I couldn't think of any other way to fix this issue without completely redesigning the level. 
It ain't pretty, but it works I guess. This also started something I came to regret, blind mazes. These are used alot in the episode and were popular to use back when I first made this. Now, I hate them
and wish I came up with something better and less frustrating. This level also starts with me using invisible tiles you need to shoot, another thing I wish I never used. While I still like the idea, it's
just too easy to get yourself stuck then have to JJK to get unstuck. Maybe this is something angelscripting could have solved if I ever learned it. The ending of the level really showcases one of my biggest
 problems. I usually went in with no plan and only a idea of which tilesets I wanted to use. This leads to stuff like the random transition at the end of the level, something I hoped to stop if I ever 
started episode 3 or 4.

-Chrysalis Caves-
I just wanted an excuse to use this tileset. Another fun fact this level was COMPLETELY redone. The original version used a MUCH older and more basic version of the tileset. After the level was done,
VioletCLM made a MUCH better version. So I painstakingly converted the level to the new tileset and then went to town. As for why you start the level falling, I don't know I was weird as a kid.
The level is House of Shells but more focused. You still have multiple objectives, but I figured a few objectives that took a while to get to was more fun then a bunch of short ones. I tired to make
this level huge, but it ended up just being mostly empty space. I intended to VASTLY expand the hoverboard section but just never got around to it and really wish I did. I think I also went a little
overboard with the eyecandy, but man is this tileset just too much fun to use. Due to the size of the level I've actually ran into bugs with tiles randomly not generating which I never found a fix for.

-Deserto-
Can you tell I enjoyed the Invasion of Deserto? This level is the same idea as Chrysalis but Deserto. I again attempted a few new ideas but nothing revolutionary. I wish I made more use of Sucker
Jumping for progression instead of just some coins. The boss fight at the end was originally going to be more involved but I think by the end I was getting fatigue from making the level and just decided 
it needed to end. I'm really glad JJ2+ added a bottemless pit event, making them work in the stock game is a nightmare and originally overloaded the events, causing non of them to spawn.

-Shipwreck-
I really enjoyed trying to use the beach tileset for something different. This whole level started on the idea of changing the water levels, but I really wish I made it longer. I had trouble thinking
of creative ways to block the player using the water level and incorporating it into the level's puzzles. As such this ends up being a short level if you know where you're going. That’s actually
why the rightmost area is just a big open space, I had no idea what to put there and is another area I wish I went back and tired to add more.

-Coast Bossfight-
This level was another late addition. The original level was blow up TNT blocks, fight boss on flat arena, done. I much later went forward and tired to make it more interesting. This whole area I wish I 
re-designed more before I lost interest, as it wasn't playtested much. I can beat it myself, but I feel it could have been more if I made the decision earlier. Also I wish I found a more elegant solution
to the TNT problem. Using TNT to block paths is nice and all, but since TNT can break ANY breakable block I had to make sure you use all of it when the game calls for it. This leads to
less then elegant solutions like using JJK to restock. Another problem angelscripting might have solved.

-Baddy Breeding-
This level was originally a long hallway with respawning enemies and that's it. I quickly realized that was a boring idea, and this is where the main structure of the story started taking place. Top Secret
is one of my favorite tilesets and I just had a blast seeing how many cool pieces of eyecandy I could make. This level is also where I think I settled on what I wanted to do going forward. The level
is actually linear, but still allows you to explore small parts of the different areas in an attempt to give off the impression of openess. If I would have made episode 3 and 4, that is the design I
probs would have leaned towards. It also includes an invisible maze, but one where you can actually see your player. Still not ideal, but better. 

-Chemical Lab-
This "Level" saddens me greatly. This level was originally going to be another true level and not a story level, going around destroying vials and support pillars to bring the whole place down. The story beats
going to be spread across the level as you started destroying the facility. As to why this is a story level, truthfully I was just done with this pack. By the time I got here I had spent literal years
making what I had to this point and was at the point I just wanted to wrap it up and try to find playtesters. If I didn't spend so much time on the earlier levels going back, changing stuff
and trying to make everything "perfect" this might have been a full level. As it stands, its just a story level, nothing else to say.

-The Unfinished Boss Fight-
This is the only level that I never finished. The original iteration of this fight was a robot fight you needed the electro blaster to damage, with ammo being fed to you slowly through a coveyer belt.
This iteration went to a tester I used to have who thought it was slow and boring, which looking back I agree. I then scrapped the whole level which is unfortunately lost to time. Something like 10 years ago
I drew up plans on a piece of paper for what I wanted the new iteration to be. It was going to start with an obstacle course platforming challenge with lasers, enemies and rotating platforms. After surviving
the gauntlet, you would be placed in a room with the main reactor. You would have no way of damaging the reactor with what you had, and all the while you would have been assaulted with tuff bosses, possibly 
blisey fireballs and other obstacles. After some frantic platforming and puzzle solving, you would have gotten access to the laser shield, which would have been used to destroy the core and also tied
into the story near the end of episode 4. As it stands, this is just a filler level and a complete anti-clmiax as I never could find the motivation to make this level.

-The Story-
The story originally started out as a remaking/reimagining of the original game's story. I decided shortly into this pack that was lame and decided to come up with my own. The
story of episode 2 changed about 20 times over the years until I had a rough draft of what all 4 episodes were going to be. Episode 2's story is still fairly basic as it was
only near the end of the episode I started implementing the bigger picture I had. My memory is hazy on what the final draft was, but this is as close to it as I can remember.
Episode 3: After dying at the hands of Devan, the player would arrive in Heaven (I REALLY wanted an excuse to use Agama's Heaven tileset). There the player would learn about 
the 2 gems, created thousands of years ago. Forged as part of a peace deal by an unknown being on earth, earth being caught in the crossfire in a war between
Heaven and Hell. The gem of Hell held immense power and was able to destroy anything in it's path. The gem of Heaven held immense protective properties, able of shielding
from even the strongest of attacks. They were given to the leaders of Heaven and Hell as part of a truce mediated by a being on earth. While this truce lasted for some time,
eventually both sides started fighting again, and in the battles both gems were lost to earth. The player then learns that Eva's ring which Devan stole in episode 1 was the gem
of hell, and they need to find the gem of Heaven. However Heaven is attacked but Hell, using the power of the gem to force open a temporary connection. Using this, the player
descends to hell and fights there way through to the leader, Satan. The player wins, but does not kill Satan, as he states that someone has to lead. Whoever defeats him will become
him and be corrupted. The player instead trades Satan's life for passage back to earth. This allows the player to emerge from the rubble of the destroyed laboratory from episode 2.
There they find a still usable computer and after some "hacking" (AKA using NOKA's Letni tileset) they find some clues to a temple possibly housing the lost gem, along with the
location of a base Devan plans on using the gem in as part of Operation Firestorm. After a few levels they find the gem only for Devan to swoop in and take it after a fight. As the 
player leaves in defeat, they notice something on the wall.
Episode 4: On the wall of the temple, an image of the gems can we been, however there are 3 instead of 2. In inscription also depicts a turtle which looks oddly similar to
Devan. Since the power of the Gem of Heaven is said to be impassible, the base as discovered in episode 3 is out of the question. With no other leads, the player returns to Devan's
house from episode 2. After some exploring, they find a previously unfound passage underneath the house, blocked by a barrier which they somehow dispelled. Through some dusty catacombs,
the player finds the third gem they saw in the engraving. From documents lying around, they learn this gem of Earth while powerless on it's own, it able to neutralize and absorb the powers of 
the other two gems. They also learn that the creator of the gems is an ancestor from Devan's family line. Confused, the player takes the gem and leaves. They raid the base and learn
about Operation Firestorm. The gem of Hell powers an orbital laser which will be used to blast and fry all life on earth. The gem of Heaven will be used on the earthbound base to
protect the earth itself from the blast, leaving nothing but a barren wasteland of death and fire. These conditions would allow Hell to effortlessly take and use earth as a staging
ground against Heaven. Learning about Devan's affiliation, the player uses the gem of earth to disable and take the gem of Heaven. They then find a teleporter to the orbital station, 
where they do the same to the Gem of Hell. With the station collapsing, the player evacuates, fighting Devan again on the way back to Earth. The player lands finding Devan defeated.
Just as it seems the story ends, Satan makes his final play in the confusion and steals all three gems from the player. The player then gets the full story. The gem of earth was made 
in secret in case either side became too powerful. The gem was used during the second war after the truce which lead to both gems becoming lost. However only the leaders of Heaven and Hell
knew this, and chose to keep it a secret. Knowing the power of this third gem, Satan used his influence to have the Rabbits go to war with the Turtles to attempt to bait out the owner 
of the gem. A young Devan, not knowing his families linage at the time, offered his services to Satan in exchange for ending the war on his people. Satan accepted, knowing Devan was
connected to the gem of earth. However Devan was unable to find it due to the barrier. The barrier could only be passed by someone of pure heart, who was unaffiliated with either Heaven or Hell.
And so Satan used Devan and created Operation Firestorm as both a way to advance his realm, and as a way of baiting the player. With any luck, the player would find out about the third gem
and attempt to retrieve it. Even the player dying was part of the plan, as it allowed them to learn of the existence of the gems without direct intervention. Satan then combines the power of all three gems 
and become invincible and all powerful. Just as all hope is lost, Devan awakens, seemingly free from Satan’s influence. Despite being a pawn, Devan was able to secretly fight the influence of Satan and 
create a secret weapon in the case of this event. This ends up being the experimental weapon used at the end of episode 2. Being unstable it would only be used for short periods of time during the final
fight, with the weapon overloading the power of the three gems and causing them to split. The player collects and the gems and as they're about to finish Satan, are again reminded that they will
become him if they do so. However this time, with the combined power of the three gems, Devan thinks the player would be shielded from the influence of Hell. They would still need to lead Hell,
however they could do so by keep it in check for all eternity. The player uses the power of the gems to destroy Satan and become the leader of Hell. The power of the gems shielding them from
Hell's influence they dedicate the rest of their existence to keeping hell under control, permanently ending the war between Heaven and Hell.
